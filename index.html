These values are near average for this time of year, and indicate that enough moisture will remain in place in the low levels to support some isolated convective development each afternoon. One of the bigger concerns with these isolated convective cells is the potential for strong to occasionally severe thunderstorm wind gusts occurring as the cells entrain the dry mid-level air into their updrafts. Evaporative cooling within the updraft could occur, and this could support a few microburst events each day as the cooler and heavier air exceeds the updraft strength resulting in thunderstorm collapse. DCAPE values of 1100 to 1300 J/KG support this concern. All of the medium range guidance is in good agreement that reinforcing and much stronger northern stream trough will dig into the eastern seaboard on Thursday and Friday. This will drive a very strong front for late July through the region Wednesday evening into Wednesday night. Behind the front, a very dry airmass as noted by PWATS falling to around an inch, or near the 10th percentile for this time of year, will move into the area. This will effectively kill off any rain chances due to a lack of moisture and instability. Although temperatures will remain near average, heat index values will fall back below advisory levels and range closer to 100 degrees on Thursday and Friday.
