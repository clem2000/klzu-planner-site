(This afternoon through Wednesday) Issued at 239 PM EDT Tue Jul 21 2026 Interplay between deep, tropical moisture streaming off of Tropical Storm Bertha (and its eventual remnants) and an approaching cold front will drive sensible weather over the next few days. As alluded to in the previous forecast, today will largely be transitional as key features settle into place. An expansive cu field has developed across north and central Georgia, and isolated to scattered thunderstorms embedded within banding on the periphery of Bertha will translate very nearly due north this afternoon. Rain rates on the order of 1-2" per hour (and perhaps up to 3" in pockets) have been observed, but comparably quick storm motions (20-30 mph) should preclude much more than nuisance flooding this afternoon. Most locales north of I-20 will remain dry. Moving into Wednesday proper, diurnally-enhanced convection will develop into the afternoon, with coverage bolstered by lift supplied from the aforementioned approaching cold front. PWATs are progged to surge above 2" (approaching daily maximums climatologically), enough to support locally torrential rainfall in the heaviest showers and thunderstorms that form. Both HiRes and global model guidance suggests that redevelopment will occur along the plume of moisture being advected atop the stalling front -- expected to be oriented roughly parallel to the I-20/I-85 connector. The potential for training/back-building along with high rain rates has prompted WPC to hoist a Slight (level 2 out of 4) Risk for Excessive Rainfall Wednesday through Thursday morning. Current thinking is that flash flooding is unlikely to be widespread, but quick responses are possible in areas that receive multiple deluges, and chances will increase after dark. Owing to progressively increasing cloud cover/storm chances, highs today are a tick cooler than yesterday, and tomorrow will be just a few degrees cooler still (relatively speaking) for the northern half of the forecast area. Highs will rise into the upper-80s to mid-90s 90s this afternoon, and into the mid-80s to lower 90s (and mid-90s for far southeast central Georgia most likely to be unaffected by convection) on Wednesday. Lows remain warm, in the low-to-mid 70s.
